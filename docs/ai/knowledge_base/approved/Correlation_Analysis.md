## 1. Why Correlation Analysis Matters

Correlation analysis helps traders and investors understand **capital flows** within crypto and between crypto and traditional markets. It answers questions like:

- "Is money rotating from Bitcoin into altcoins?"
- "Is crypto correlated with stocks this week, or decoupling?"
- "Are stablecoins flowing in (bullish) or out (bearish)?"
- "Will a Fed rate hike crash the market?"
- "Are we in an alt season or a BTC dominance phase?"

**Core principle**: Correlation does not equal causation. Two assets moving together does not mean one causes the other. Correlation can break down during extreme volatility, regime changes, or liquidity shocks. Always look for **convergence of multiple independent signals** before drawing conclusions.

## 2. Bitcoin Dominance (BTC.D) — The Market's Risk Compass

### 2.1 What BTC.D Measures

Bitcoin Dominance = (Bitcoin Market Cap) / (Total Crypto Market Cap) × 100

It represents Bitcoin's share of the total cryptocurrency market. When BTC.D rises, capital is flowing into Bitcoin faster than altcoins. When BTC.D falls, capital is rotating into altcoins.

**Current range**: Typically 38%–73% historically. Peaked around 73% in late 2020, bottomed near 38% in late 2021. Exact values vary by data source (CoinMarketCap vs CoinGecko vs TradingView).

### 2.2 The Dominance Cycle

BTC.D moves in long-term cycles (years) that correlate with broader market phases:

| Phase | BTC.D Direction | Market Behavior |
|---|---|---|
| **Bear accumulation** | Rising | Capitulation ends → BTC holds value better than alts → "flight to quality" within crypto |
| **Early bull** | Falling | BTC rallies first, then alts start outperforming → rotated capital enters alts |
| **Peak euphoria** | Falling sharply | Alts massively outperform BTC → meme coins, low-cap pumps → BTC.D near lows |
| **Distribution** | Rising sharply | Alts crash harder than BTC → "flight to safety" back to BTC → BTC.D surges |
| **Bear capitulation** | Peaking / sideways | Everything crashes → BTC loses less → BTC.D stays elevated |

**Key insight**: BTC.D is NOT a tradable asset. It's a **sentiment and flow indicator**. The cycle of BTC.D rising then falling maps to the risk-on/risk-off pulse of the entire crypto market.

### 2.3 Dominance Phases Through Market Cycles

**Historically observed pattern** (2017 cycle, 2021 cycle):

1. **BTC slowly rises** → BTC.D increases (or stays flat) — BTC leads.
2. **BTC breaks out to new highs** → BTC.D starts falling — profit rotates to alts.
3. **ETH BTC pair rallies** → Alt season begins — capital flows from BTC to ETH to small-caps.
4. **Small-cap alts explode** → BTC.D drops sharply — euphoria phase.
5. **BTC.D bottom** → Market peak imminent — liquidity exhausted.
6. **Alts crash** → BTC.D surges — "only Bitcoin survives."
7. **Bear market** → BTC.D stays elevated for months.

Cycle 2021 was compressed (4-year cycle accelerated by COVID liquidity). The next cycle may follow a similar pattern but with different drivers.

### 2.4 How to Read BTC.D Movement

| BTC.D Moving | Altcoins Doing | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| **Up** | Underperforming BTC | Risk-off within crypto. Capital parking in BTC. |
| **Down** | Outperforming BTC | Risk-on. Capital rotating into alts. |
| **Steady at lows** | Mixed | Market may be topping. No fresh capital entering alts. |
| **Steady at highs** | Mixed | Bear market bottoming process. BTC dominance peaks. |
| **Sharp up** | Crashing | Alt capitulation. "Only Bitcoin is safe" — often near local bottoms for alts. |

## 3. Altcoin Season Indicators

### 3.1 Altcoin Season Index

The Altcoin Season Index (popularized by CoinMarketCap) measures the percentage of the top 100 coins outperforming Bitcoin over a rolling period (typically 90 days):

- **0–25**: Bitcoin Season. BTC dominates. Alts bleed.
- **25–50**: Mixed. Some alt sectors active, but not broad.
- **50–75**: Approaching Alt Season. Alts gaining relative strength.
- **75–100**: Alt Season. Majority of top 100 outperforming BTC.

**How the AI should use this**: When interpreting a user's "should I buy alts?" or "is alt season here?" question, reference the season index. If below 40, tell the user that broad alt outperformance is unlikely. If above 60, note that alts are showing strength.

### 3.2 ETH/BTC Pair — The Alt Season Bellwether

The ETH/BTC price ratio is the single most watched indicator for alt season:

- **ETH/BTC rising** → ETH outperforming BTC → Capital flows from BTC → ETH → smaller alts.
- **ETH/BTC falling** → ETH underperforming BTC → Capital retreating from ETH to BTC → Alts generally weak.
- **ETH/BTC breakout above long-term resistance** → Historically, precedes major alt rallies.
- **ETH/BTC breakdown below support** → Alts likely to suffer disproportionately.

**Why ETH?** ETH is the largest "alt" and acts as the base pair for much of DeFi and L2 ecosystems. When ETH rallies against BTC, it unlocks capital for the rest of the alt market. This is often called the "rising tide lifts all boats" phenomenon within crypto.

**Note for the AI**: ETH/BTC ratio has been in a multi-year downtrend since late 2021 after peaking near 0.085. This roughly coincided with the merge and subsequent landscape shift where BTC started outperforming ETH. A sustained reversal of this downtrend would be a major alt-season signal.

### 3.3 Top 50 Performance vs BTC

Track the percentage of the top 50 coins by market cap that are outperforming BTC over rolling periods:

- **7 days**: Short-term momentum reading. Noisy but useful for early signals.
- **30 days**: Medium-term trend. Sustained outperformance suggests alt rotation underway.
- **90 days**: Reference read. Most commonly cited in alt season analysis.

### 3.4 Exchange Inflow Dynamics

- **ETH flowing into exchanges** on net → Potential selling pressure on ETH → Negative for ETH/BTC and alt season.
- **Stablecoins flowing into exchanges** on net → Buying power building → Usually precedes alt buying.
- **BTC flowing into exchanges** → Potential BTC selling → Could be profit-taking or capitulation.

### 3.5 Stablecoin Exchange Reserves

The amount of USDT, USDC, and BUSD sitting on exchanges represents **dry powder** for buying:

- **High exchange stablecoin reserves** → Buying power for the next leg up.
- **Declining exchange stablecoin reserves** → Buying power being deployed (bullish if market is rising, potentially bearish if reserves are depleted).
- **Rising exchange stablecoin reserves during a downtrend** → Buying power accumulating — potential for a bounce.
- **Falling exchange stablecoin reserves during an uptrend** → Ammunition being spent — trend may be exhausting.

## 4. Cross-Asset Correlation Matrix

Crypto is increasingly correlated with traditional macro assets, particularly since 2020.

### 4.1 BTC / S&P 500 (SPX)

| Period | Approx. Correlation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2019 | 0.0 to 0.2 | Near-zero. Crypto was uncorrelated. |
| 2020–2022 | 0.5 to 0.8 | Strong correlation. COVID liquidity drove both. |
| 2023–present | 0.3 to 0.6 | Correlation persists but weakening. |

**When correlation is high** (above 0.5): BTC trades like high-beta tech. A 1% SPX drop = ~2–3% BTC drop. Crypto investors must watch macro news (FOMC, CPI, NFP).

**When correlation is low** (below 0.3): BTC is driven by crypto-native factors (halving, ETF flows, stablecoin dynamics, on-chain activity). This is when alpha opportunities emerge.

**How correlation breaks**:
- During crypto-specific events (FTX collapse → crypto correlation rises but macro correlation drops).
- During macro shocks (banking crisis in March 2023 → BTC rallied as "digital gold" while stocks fell).
- During Bitcoin ETF news cycles (correlation drops as BTC trades on its own fundamentals).

### 4.2 BTC / Gold

Gold is the classic "safe haven" and inflation hedge. BTC is often called "digital gold."

| Period | Approx. Correlation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 2017–2021 | 0.0 to 0.2 | BTC treated as risk asset, gold as safe haven. |
| 2022 (crash) | 0.3 to 0.5 | Both fell on rate hikes. Correlation rose. |
| 2023 (banking crisis) | −0.3 to 0.3 | Low/correlated. BTC challenged gold narrative during banking stress. |

**Key insight**: BTC has NOT consistently behaved as "digital gold." During most risk-off events, BTC drops with stocks. The digital gold narrative is aspirational, not consistently proven. However, in specific circumstances (banking crisis, currency debasement fears), BTC has shown gold-like behavior for brief periods.

**For the AI**: Always qualify BTC/gold correlation claims with timeframe context. The "digital gold" label is a narrative, not an established trading relationship.

### 4.3 BTC / DXY (US Dollar Index)

DXY measures USD strength against a basket of major currencies (EUR, JPY, GBP, etc.).

- **Strong dollar (DXY rising)** → Typically negative for BTC and crypto.
  - Tight financial conditions reduce liquidity.
  - USD-denominated assets attract capital away from speculative assets.
  - Emerging markets (major crypto adoption regions) suffer.

- **Weak dollar (DXY falling)** → Typically positive for BTC and crypto.
  - Loose financial conditions boost liquidity.
  - Dollar debasement narrative drives capital into BTC.
  - EM currencies strengthen → crypto adoption regions recover.

**Correlation est.**: −0.3 to −0.5 (moderately inverse). Stronger during periods of dollar liquidity crises (2020, 2022).

**Important nuance**: The DXY correlation is not direct. It works through global liquidity conditions. DXY rising → global liquidity tightening → risk assets (including crypto) fall. The relationship is mediated by central bank policies.

### 4.4 BTC / ETH (Intra-Crypto Pairs)

| Pair | Typical Correlation | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| BTC / ETH | 0.6–0.85 | Strong but ETH has higher beta (~1.2–1.5× BTC). |
| ETH / SOL | 0.5–0.75 | Both L1s but diverging narratives and ecosystems. |
| BTC / alt | 0.4–0.7 | Lower correlation, especially for small alts with distinct ecosystem drivers. |

**Beta example**: If BTC rallies 10% and BTC/ETH correlation is 0.8 with ETH beta 1.3, ETH would be expected to rally roughly 10% × 0.8 × 1.3 = 10.4% — a slightly amplified move in the same direction.

**When correlation drops to 0.5 or below**: One of the assets is developing a unique catalyst-driven trajectory. E.g., ETH on the merge announcement created its own momentum independent of BTC.

### 4.5 Total Crypto Market Cap / Global M2 Liquidity

Global M2 (broad money supply) is a proxy for total liquidity in the financial system.

- **M2 expanding** → Liquidity injections → Risk assets rise → Crypto normally follows with a lag of 2–6 months.
- **M2 contracting** → Liquidity draining → Risk assets fall → Crypto normally follows.

**Historical observation (correlation est.: 0.4–0.6 with 3-month lag)**:
- 2020: M2 exploded (COVID stimulus) → Crypto exploded ~3 months later.
- 2022: M2 growth reversed (rate hikes) → Crypto crashed.
- 2023–2024: M2 growth stabilized then began rising → Crypto recovered.

**Leading indicators**: Central bank balance sheets (Fed, ECB, BOJ, PBOC), RRP facility usage, reverse repo balances. When central banks inject, crypto eventually rises. When they drain, crypto eventually falls.

## 5. Stablecoin Flows — The On-Ramp Signal

### 5.1 Minting and Burning

- **Minting** (new USDT/USDC created) → New capital entering the crypto ecosystem. Bullish.
- **Burning** (USDT/USDC redeemed) → Capital exiting crypto. Bearish.
- **Net minting sustained over weeks** → Accumulation phase.
- **Net minting slowing while prices rise** → Late-cycle signal.

**Where to track**: Tether Transparency page, USDC Circle's attestation reports, Dune Analytics dashboards.

### 5.2 Exchange Reserve Tracking

The amount of stablecoins held on exchanges is **immediate buying power**:

- **High and rising** → Buyers are parked, waiting. Potential fuel for rallies.
- **High and declining** → Buying power being deployed. If price rising → bullish. If price falling → buyers providing support.
- **Low and declining** → Buying power exhausted. Market may top or correct.
- **Low and rising** → Capital slowly returning. Early accumulation phase.

### 5.3 Stablecoin Dominance (USDT.D, USDC.D)

Stablecoin Dominance = (Total Stablecoin Market Cap) / (Total Crypto Market Cap) × 100

Similar to BTC.D, stablecoin dominance measures how much capital is in stablecoins vs volatile crypto:

- **USDT.D rising** → Capital retreating to stablecoins → Risk-off. Capital preservation mode.
- **USDT.D falling** → Capital moving from stablecoins into crypto → Risk-on. Deployment mode.

**Relationship with BTC.D**: BTC.D and USDT.D often rise together during bear markets (flight to both BTC and stables). But there's nuance: rising BTC.D + falling USDT.D = capital rotating from stables directly into BTC (new buying). Rising BTC.D + rising USDT.D = capital fleeing both BTC and alts into cash.

### 5.4 Capital Rotation Between Stablecoins

Different stablecoins serve different purposes:
- **USDT**: Most traded. Preferred in Asia, emerging markets. Highest on-chain velocity.
- **USDC**: Regulatory compliance. Preferred by institutions. More transparent reserves.
- **DAI/FRAX**: DeFi-native. Used in yield farming, lending protocols.

**Tracking flows**: Flow from USDT to USDC suggests institutional positioning. Flow from USDC to USDT suggests retail accumulation. Flow from stables into DAI suggests DeFi activity increasing.

## 6. Macro Correlations

### 6.1 Federal Reserve Policy

The most impactful macro factor on crypto since 2020:

**Rate decisions**:
- **Rate hikes** → Liquidity tightening → Risk assets (crypto) fall → typically leads to 6–12 months of bearish crypto.
- **Rate cuts** → Liquidity easing → Risk assets rise → typically leads to crypto rallies.
- **Rate pause** → Market stabilizes → Crypto may bottom.

**Quantitative Tightening (QT)** → Fed reducing balance sheet → Drains liquidity → Negative for crypto.
**Quantitative Easing (QE)** → Fed expanding balance sheet → Injects liquidity → Positive for crypto.

**Forward guidance**: The market prices expectations, not current rates. Crypto often rallies on "dovish surprises" (less hawkish than expected) and falls on "hawkish surprises" (more aggressive than expected).

**For the AI**: When users ask "why did crypto dump?", check if there was a recent FOMC meeting, CPI print, or Fed speaker hawkish statement. These drive short-term crypto price action far more than crypto-native factors during high-correlation regimes.

### 6.2 Global Liquidity Cycles

Beyond just the Fed, global central bank liquidity drives crypto:

- **Bank of Japan (BOJ)**: Yield curve control changes affect global yields. BOJ tightening = global liquidity drain.
- **People's Bank of China (PBOC)**: Chinese stimulus affects global M2. PBOC easing = global liquidity boost.
- **European Central Bank (ECB)**: Euro liquidity matters for offshore BTC trading (e.g., Coinbase Europe, Bitstamp).

**Best indicator**: Global M2 money supply (USD-adjusted aggregate). When M2 growth turns positive, crypto typically rallies within 3 months.

### 6.3 Inflation and Real Yields

**Real Yields** = Nominal Bond Yields − Expected Inflation

- **Real yields rising** → Everything risk-off. Cash and bonds become competitive.
- **Real yields falling** → Risk-on. "There is no alternative" (TINA) drives capital into risk assets.
- **Real yields deeply negative** (e.g., 2020–2021) → FOMO into hard assets → BTC rallies.

**Inflation** (CPI, PCE) → The primary variable:
- **High inflation** → Fed must hike → Bad for crypto short-term.
- **But... high inflation also** → Undermines fiat confidence → Potentially good for BTC long-term if it becomes an inflation hedge.
- **Falling inflation** → Fed can cut → Good for crypto.

### 6.4 Geopolitical Risk

Geopolitical events have mixed effects on crypto:

| Event Type | Typical Crypto Reaction |
|---|---|
| **War / conflict** | Initially drops (risk-off), then may recover if it creates fiat instability |
| **Banking crisis** | Initially drops, then may rally as "decentralization" narrative strengthens |
| **Debt ceiling crisis** | Brief dip, then recovery. May accelerate BTC adoption narrative. |
| **Regulation (positive)** | Rally in affected sectors. E.g., ETF approval → BTC rallies. |
| **Regulation (negative)** | Sharp drop. E.g., China ban → crash then recovery. |
| **Sanctions** | Complex. May increase demand for censorship-resistant money (BTC). |

## 7. Sector Rotation Within Crypto

Money flows between crypto sectors in identifiable cycles. Tracking this helps the AI answer "which coins are hot right now?" and "should I rotate my portfolio?"

### 7.1 Layer 1 / Smart Contract Platforms

- **Included**: ETH, SOL, AVAX, ADA, DOT, NEAR, FTM, MATIC/POL
- **Phase in cycle**: Early bull. L1s are the first alts to outperform BTC.
- **On-chain signal**: TVL rising, active addresses rising, DEX volume rising.
- **Trading**: Buy L1s early in bull, sell as dominance shifts to L2s/application layers.

### 7.2 Layer 2 / Scaling

- **Included**: ARB, OP, BASE ecosystem tokens, STRK, METIS
- **Phase in cycle**: Mid bull. After L1s run, L2s catch up.
- **On-chain signal**: L2 TVL rising, L1 base fees declining relative to L2 activity.
- **Trading**: L2 tokens tend to have strong narratives but lower float → high volatility.

### 7.3 DeFi (Decentralized Finance)

- **Included**: UNI, AAVE, CRV, MKR, COMP, SUSHI, BAL
- **Phase in cycle**: Mid bull. After L1 infrastructure is established, capital seeks yield.
- **On-chain signal**: TVL growth in lending and DEX protocols, yield spreads widening.
- **Trading**: DeFi outperforms when yields are attractive and market sentiment is "risk-on."

### 7.4 Gaming and Metaverse

- **Included**: SAND, MANA, AXS, GALA, IMX, YGG
- **Phase in cycle**: Late bull. Capital rotates to speculative gaming tokens near cycle peak.
- **On-chain signal**: Game-related wallet activity, in-game token volume.
- **Warning**: These are high-beta, often decouple from fundamentals. Most vulnerable during bear markets.

### 7.5 AI and DePIN

- **Included**: RNDR/Akash (render), FIL/AR (storage), TAO (AI), GRT (indexing)
- **Phase in cycle**: Narrative-driven. Pumps on tech announcements and broader AI hype.
- **Correlation**: RNDR/TAO correlate with NASDAQ AI stocks (NVDA, MSFT) more than BTC.
- **Trading**: Trade them like tech stocks, not like crypto. Watch NVIDIA earnings.

### 7.6 Meme Coins and Cult Communities

- **Included**: DOGE, SHIB, PEPE, WIF, BONK, DOG
- **Phase in cycle**: Late bull / peak euphoria. "Only goes up" narratives.
- **Correlation**: Very low with BTC. Driven by social sentiment, exchange listings, influencer shilling.
- **Warning**: Extremely high risk. No fundamental value. Most lose 90%+ in bear markets.

### 7.7 How to Track Sector Rotation

Use a combination of:
1. **Dual Moving Averages** (50/200 SMA) on sector indices.
2. **Relative Strength vs BTC** — Which sectors are outperforming BTC?
3. **Trading volume** — Rising volume confirms sector participation.
4. **TVL / Active users** — On-chain data confirms if capital is really flowing.

## 8. Correlation Breakdowns — When Pairs Decouple

### 8.1 During Black Swan Events

Examples: COVID crash (March 2020), FTX collapse (Nov 2022), LUNA de-peg (May 2022).

During black swans:
- **All correlations tend toward +1.0** — everything dumps together.
- **Safe haven assets** (Gold, USD, Treasuries) may rally while everything else crashes.
- **BTC and altcoin correlation approaches 0.9+** — no diversification benefit.

**For the AI**: During a black swan, tell users that correlation breaks down and nearly all risk assets will move together. Hedging is extremely difficult.

### 8.2 During Liquidity Crises

Examples: March 2020, September 2019 repo market crisis.

All correlations rise because everything is driven by the same factor: availability of dollar liquidity.

**Warning signal**: When crypto starts correlating with everything (stocks, bonds, commodities, gold all moving together), it's a sign of systemic liquidity stress.

### 8.3 During Regime Changes

Example: 2021 crypto bull peak → BTC.D stopped falling → Alt season ended.

Regime changes happen when:
- The primary driver of correlation (liquidity, sentiment, narrative) changes.
- BTC.D changes direction.
- A new catalyst emerges (ETF approval, regulatory clarity, halving).

**Detecting regime change**: Look for BTC diverging from SPX, or ETH/BTC breaking a long-term trend.

### 8.4 Altcoin Decoupling During "Alt Season"

During true alt seasons, small-cap alts decouple from BTC:
- BTC may trade sideways while alts rally 50–200%.
- Correlation between BTC and small-cap tokens drops from ~0.6 to ~0.2.
- This is the most profitable time for alt traders and the most dangerous time for latecomers.

**Pattern**: BTC stabilizes → ETH/BTC rises → mid-cap alts rally → small-cap alts explode → BTC.D bottoms → alt season ends.

## 9. On-Chain Correlation Validation

Cross-reference correlation signals with on-chain data to confirm whether the flow is real.

### 9.1 Network Activity vs Price

- **Price rising + active addresses rising** → Organic demand. Bullish.
- **Price rising + active addresses flat** → Speculative. Less sustainable.
- **Price falling + active addresses rising** → Accumulation. Potential bottom.
- **Price falling + active addresses falling** → Capitulation. Bottom not in.

### 9.2 Whale Accumulation vs Retail Flow

- **Whale wallets accumulating + retail selling** → Smart money buying. Bullish divergence.
- **Whale wallets distributing + retail buying** → Smart money selling. Bearish divergence.

**Data source**: Glassnode, Santiment, Nansen wallet labeling. Track wallets with >1,000 BTC or >10,000 ETH.

### 9.3 Realized Cap and HODL Waves

**Realized Cap**: Market cap weighted by each coin's last moved price (vs current price). Shows aggregate cost basis.

**HODL Waves**: Distribution of coin supply by age (1d–1wk, 1wk–1m, 1m–1y, 1y+).

- **Old coins moving** (1y+ spent) → Long-term holders selling → Potential top signal.
- **Young coins accumulating** (1d–1wk shrinking) → Long-term holders holding → Bottoming process.
- **Realized Cap rising** → New capital entering at higher prices → Healthy trend.
- **Realized Cap falling** → Capital exiting → Bearish.

## 10. Trading Strategies Using Correlation

### 10.1 Pair Trading

Enter two correlated assets, long the stronger one, short the weaker:

- **Long ETH / Short BTC** when ETH/BTC ratio is expected to rise.
- **Long alts / Short BTC** when alt season is expected.
- **Long BTC / Short alt** when BTC.D is expected to rise (risk-off).

**Risk**: Correlation breakdown. If the pair decouples in an unexpected direction, both legs lose.

### 10.2 Beta Hedging

If you're long a high-beta altcoin and expect a market-wide pullback:

1. Determine alt's beta to BTC (e.g., 1.5×).
2. Determine desired portfolio exposure (e.g., $10,000 long SOL).
3. Short equivalent BTC exposure: $10,000 × 1.5 = $15,000 BTC short.
4. If market drops 10%, SOL falls ~15%, BTC falls 10%. Net loss: $10,000 × −15% + $15,000 × +10% = −$1,500 + $1,500 = $0 (neutralized).

**Note**: Beta is not stable. It changes with market conditions. Requires active management.

### 10.3 Sector Rotation Timing

Track sector indices (e.g., BINO DeFi Index, BINO Gaming Index) vs BTC:

1. Identify which sector is outperforming BTC (strongest relative strength).
2. Wait for pullback in that sector while BTC is stable.
3. Enter long on the pullback.
4. Exit when sector rotation shifts (new sector starts outperforming).

### 10.4 Dominance Breakout Trading

Trade the BTC.D level breakouts/breakdowns:

- **BTC.D breaking above resistance** → Go long BTC, short alts.
- **BTC.D breaking below support** → Go long alts, short BTC.
- **BTC.D rejecting at key level** → Reversal trade in the opposite direction.

**Confirmation**: Wait for BTC.D move to be accompanied by volume (market cap data). Divergence between BTC.D and price can signal false breakouts.

## 11. Important Caveats and Limitations

1. **Correlation changes over time.** A relationship that held last year may not hold today. Always check recent data.
2. **Correlation does not imply causation.** Two assets moving together does NOT mean the same capital is flowing between them.
3. **Lagged correlations exist.** M2 → Crypto has a ~3 month lag. Don't expect immediate reaction.
4. **Narrative-driven moves break correlations.** A positive catalyst for one coin (e.g., ETF approval) will decouple it from correlated assets temporarily.
5. **Correlation is higher in bear markets.** When uncertainty is high, everything trades as "risk-on / risk-off."
6. **Correlation is lower in bull markets.** Alpha opportunities increase when correlation drops.
7. **Data quality varies.** BTC.D differs across data sources. OI data is exchange-specific. Stablecoin supply figures may include non-circulating supply.
8. **On-chain data is always backward-looking.** It tells you what happened, not what will happen. The largest wallets move last.
9. **Stablecoin flows can be distorted.** A large minting event might be for business reasons (exchange adding liquidity) rather than new retail capital.
10. **The AI must not present correlation as tradeable certainty.** Always frame as: "Correlations suggest... but capital flows can reverse instantly on new information."
