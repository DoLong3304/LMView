## 1. How to Use This Encyclopedia

Each pattern entry follows a consistent structure:

**1. Description** — What the pattern looks like and what it signals.
**2. Identification Rules** — Specific geometric and volume criteria.
**3. Volume Confirmation** — How volume should behave during pattern formation and breakout.
**4. Trading Implications** — Entry, stop, target, and reliability notes.
**5. Failure Conditions** — What would invalidate the pattern.

**For the AI**: Use these rules to identify patterns on charts. Do not force a pattern onto a chart that doesn't meet criteria. A "failed pattern" (e.g., a head and shoulders that doesn't break the neckline) often signals the opposite move.

## 2. Reversal Patterns

### 2.1 Head and Shoulders (H&S) / Inverse H&S

**Description**: A bearish reversal pattern (at market tops) consisting of three peaks: a higher middle peak (head) flanked by two lower peaks (shoulders). Inverse H&S is the bullish equivalent at market bottoms.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **Prior trend**: Must follow an uptrend (H&S top) or downtrend (H&S bottom).
2. **Left shoulder**: Peak and decline — volume typically high.
3. **Head**: Price exceeds left shoulder peak — volume typically lower than left shoulder.
4. **Right shoulder**: Lower peak than the head — volume typically lowest of the three.
5. **Neckline**: Trendline connecting the two troughs. Can slope up, down, or sideways.
6. **Breakdown/Breakout**: Price must close below (H&S top) or above (H&S bottom) the neckline.
7. **Pullback**: Often the neckline is retested after the breakdown.

**Volume Confirmation**:
- Left shoulder: High volume (the run-up was active).
- Head: Lower volume (momentum slowing).
- Right shoulder: Lowest volume (buyers exhausted).
- Neckline break: Volume should spike 2×+ average.
- Pullback: Volume should be below average.
- Continuation after pullback: Volume should increase again.

| Criterion | H&S (Bearish) | Inverse H&S (Bullish) |
|---|---|---|
| Prior trend | Uptrend | Downtrend |
| Signal | Breakdown below neckline | Breakout above neckline |
| Target | Neckline − (Head to Neckline height) | Neckline + (Head to Neckline height) |
| Stop | Above right shoulder | Below right shoulder |

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On neckline break close (aggressive) or on retest of neckline (conservative).
- **Target**: Height of pattern (head to neckline) projected from break point.
- **Stop**: Above the right shoulder (H&S top) or below the right shoulder (H&S bottom).
- **Reliability**: High on weekly/daily. Moderate on lower timeframes.
- **Success rate**: ~80–85% for major tops/bottoms on daily+. Lower on intraday.

**Failure Conditions**:
- Right shoulder exceeds head → not an H&S, trend likely continuing.
- Neckline break fails (price returns inside) → "H&S failure" which often signals a powerful move in the OPPOSITE direction.
- Volume spike on right shoulder → may indicate continued buying rather than exhaustion.

### 2.2 Double Top / Double Bottom

**Description**: Two peaks (Double Top) or two troughs (Double Bottom) at roughly the same price level.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **Prior trend**: Must follow an uptrend (Double Top) or downtrend (Double Bottom).
2. **Two peaks/troughs**: Should be within 1–3% of each other.
3. **Trough between peaks** (for double top): At least 10% retracement of the first peak.
4. **Neckline**: The trough between peaks (double top) or the peak between troughs (double bottom).
5. **Break below/above neckline**: Confirms the pattern.

**Volume Confirmation**:
- First peak: High volume (climax buying/selling).
- Decline to neckline: Volume drops.
- Second peak/trough: Lower volume than first.
- Breakout from neckline: Volume should spike.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On neckline break close (aggressive) or on pullback to neckline (conservative).
- **Target**: Distance from neckline to peak/trough, projected from break point.
- **Stop**: Above the second peak (double top) or below the second trough (double bottom).
- **Success rate**: ~70–75%. Lower on tight ranges (< 10% width).

**Failure Conditions**:
- Price exceeds the second peak (double top) → pattern is a "bullish continuation" feature, not a reversal.
- Low volume during neckline break → high probability of false breakout.
- Price stalls at neckline without breaking → consolidation continuing.

### 2.3 Triple Top / Triple Bottom

**Description**: Three peaks (Triple Top) or three troughs (Triple Bottom) at roughly the same level — rarer but more powerful than double tops/bottoms.

**Identification Rules**:
1. Three touches of a horizontal S/R level with clear reversals between each.
2. Each reversal (pullback between peaks) should be a clear move (10%+).
3. Breakout/breakdown confirmed on the third touch failure.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Target**: Pattern height projected from break point.
- **Success rate**: ~85% (higher than double because the extra touch confirms the level).
- Timing: Takes longer to form, tends to precede major moves.

### 2.4 Rounding Top / Rounding Bottom

**Description**: A slow, gradual reversal forming a U-shape (bottom) or dome-shape (top). Also called a "saucer" pattern.

**Identification Rules**:
1. Price gradually slows its trend, plateaus, then slowly reverses.
2. Duration: Typically 1–6 months. Longer = more significant.
3. No sharp reversal — price gradually bends around.
4. Volume: Rises on the first half, declines in the middle, rises again on the second half.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On the breakout above/below the rounding baseline.
- **Target**: Height of the rounding pattern (the full range).
- **Success rate**: ~80% on weekly/monthly.
- **Limitation**: Hard to trade because it forms slowly and the breakout is often subtle.

### 2.5 V-Top / V-Bottom (Spike Reversal)

**Description**: A sharp reversal without a clear pattern — price goes up fast and down even faster (V-top) or drops fast and reverses sharply (V-bottom).

**Identification Rules**:
1. Sharp, almost vertical move in one direction.
2. Immediate reversal with equal momentum in the opposite direction.
3. No consolidation between the two legs.
4. Often driven by news, liquidation cascade, or squeeze.

**Trading Implications**:
- Extremely difficult to trade in real time (you don't know it's a V-top until the reversal is well underway).
- Best traded after the fact: wait for the first pullback after the V, then trade in the V-reversal direction.
- Volume spikes are extreme during the reversal.
- **Failure condition**: The V continues in the original direction (trend resumes).

### 2.6 Key Reversal Day / One-Day Reversal

**Description**: A single candle where price makes a new high/low but closes in the opposite direction.

**Identification Rules**:
1. Price opens and makes a new 20+ period high (bullish) or low (bearish).
2. Price then reverses to close below the prior day's close (bearish reversal) or above (bullish reversal).
3. The candle has a long upper wick (bearish) or lower wick (bullish).
4. Volume is above average.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On the next candle continuing in the reversal direction.
- **Stop**: Beyond the extreme of the key reversal candle.
- **Success rate**: ~65% — significant but needs follow-through confirmation.

## 3. Continuation Patterns

### 3.1 Flags and Pennants

**Description**: Short-term consolidation patterns after a sharp directional move. Flags are parallelograms; pennants are small symmetrical triangles.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **Flagpole**: A sharp, almost vertical move (the "pole").
2. **Flag**: Rectangular consolidation, slanted against the prior trend direction.
3. **Pennant**: Small triangle with converging trendlines, also against the prior trend.
4. **Duration**: 1–3 weeks (shorter on lower timeframes).
5. **Breakout**: In the direction of the prior trend with volume spike.

**Volume Confirmation**:
- Flagpole: Very high volume.
- Consolidation (flag/pennant): Volume declining.
- Breakout: Volume spikes to flagpole levels or higher.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On the breakout candle close in the prior trend direction.
- **Target**: The height of the flagpole, projected from the breakout point.
- **Stop**: Below the flag/pennant (in an uptrend) or above (downtrend).
- **Success rate**: ~85–90% for strong prior trends.
- **Failure condition**: Breakout against the prior trend → the prior move may be exhausted.

### 3.2 Wedges (Rising and Falling)

**Description**: Converging trendlines with both lines moving in the same direction. Rising Wedge is typically bearish; Falling Wedge is typically bullish.

**Identification Rules**:

**Rising Wedge (Bearish)**:
- Both trendlines slope upward, with the upper line steeper or converging.
- Typically forms after an uptrend or during a bear market rally.
- Volume declines as wedge progresses.
- Breakdown below the lower trendline confirms.

**Falling Wedge (Bullish)**:
- Both trendlines slope downward, with the lower line steeper or converging.
- Typically forms after a downtrend or during a bull market dip.
- Volume declines as wedge progresses.
- Breakout above the upper trendline confirms.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Target**: The widest part of the wedge (at the beginning), projected from the break point.
- **Success rate**: ~75%. More reliable when preceded by a strong prior trend.
- **Failure condition**: Breakout in the WRONG direction (e.g., rising wedge breaks up → false signal).

### 3.3 Triangles (Ascending, Descending, Symmetrical)

**Ascending Triangle (Bullish)**:
- **Structure**: Flat resistance top, rising support bottom.
- **Typically a continuation pattern** — breakout above resistance.
- **Volume**: Declines during formation, spikes on breakout.
- **Target**: Height of triangle (from flat resistance to first support touch), projected upward.

**Descending Triangle (Bearish)**:
- **Structure**: Flat support bottom, declining resistance top.
- **Typically a continuation pattern** — breakdown below support.
- **Volume**: Declines during formation, spikes on breakdown.
- **Target**: Height of triangle projected downward.

**Symmetrical Triangle**:
- **Structure**: Both trendlines converging. No inherent directional bias.
- **Volume**: Declines to the apex. Breakout direction determines bias.
- **Entry**: On volume-confirmed breakout.
- **Target**: Widest part of the triangle projected from breakout.
- **Reliability**: ~65–75%, lower than ascending/descending because direction is unknown.

**Common triangle failure**: Price reaches the apex without breaking out → "collapse" or lazy triangle → often a violent move in either direction.

### 3.4 Rectangles (Trading Range)

**Description**: Horizontal range between parallel support and resistance. Also called a "box" or "trading range."

**Identification Rules**:
1. At least two touches of resistance (top of range) and two touches of support (bottom of range).
2. Range width: Usually 5–15% of the price level.
3. Duration: Weeks to months. Longer ranges → larger breakouts.
4. Breakout direction: Determined by the prevailing trend (continuation) or a major S/R level break.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Range trade**: Buy at support, sell at resistance (before breakout).
- **Breakout trade**: Enter on close above/below with volume confirmation.
- **Target**: Range height projected from breakout.
- **Stop**: Inside the range (for range trades) or below the breakout candle low (for breakout).

### 3.5 Cup and Handle

**Description**: A bullish continuation pattern resembling a tea cup — U-shaped rounding bottom with a short consolidation (handle) at the top-right.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **Prior trend**: Uptrend (bullish).
2. **Cup**: Smooth U-shape, typically 1–6 months long. Should not be a V-sharp bottom.
3. **Handle**: Short consolidation (1–4 weeks) drifting downward or sideways on the upper-right of the cup.
4. **Volume**: Should decrease during the handle and spike on handle breakout.
5. **Breakout**: Above the handle resistance (usually the cup rim).

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On close above handle resistance with volume.
- **Target**: Depth of the cup projected upward from the breakout point.
- **Stop**: Below the handle low.
- **Success rate**: ~80%. More reliable on weekly/daily timeframes.

**Failure**: Handle drops below 50% of the cup → may be a double top instead of cup and handle.

### 3.6 Measured Move (Flag-like)

**Description**: A three-leg pattern: impulse move → consolidation → impulse move of similar magnitude.

**Identification Rules**:
1. First leg: Strong directional move (impulse wave).
2. Consolidation: A sideways or counter-trend pullback (flag/pennant/triangle or zigzag).
3. Second leg: Directional move of similar magnitude to the first leg.
4. The two legs are often approximately equal in price distance.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On confirmation of the second leg (breakout from the consolidation).
- **Target**: Distance of the first leg, projected from consolidation breakout.
- **Stop**: Within the consolidation.

## 4. Bilateral Patterns (Can Break Either Way)

### 4.1 Symmetrical Triangle

(See section 3.3 — described there as a bilateral pattern.)

### 4.2 Expanding Triangle / Megaphone

**Description**: Diverging trendlines — higher highs and lower lows, creating a widening range.

**Identification Rules**:
1. At least three touches on both upper (higher highs) and lower (lower lows) bounds.
2. Volume expands with each swing (unlike most patterns where volume contracts).
3. Typically forms at market tops after extended trends.
4. Can break either direction.

**Trading Implications**:
- Difficult to trade due to wide range expansion.
- Can be traded as a "range expansion" — buy at lower bound / sell at upper bound.
- Prefers a breakout in the opposite direction of the prevailing trend (reversal signal).
- **Failure**: Continued expansion with no breakout → breakdown of structure.

### 4.3 Diamond Pattern

**Description**: A rare pattern that combines a broadening formation with a symmetrical triangle — first wider, then narrower.

**Identification Rules**:
1. First half: Price makes higher highs and lower lows (expanding).
2. Second half: Price makes lower highs and higher lows (contracting).
3. Shape resembles a diamond.
4. Volume: High during the first half, declining in the second half.
5. Breakout direction: Can go either way, though diamond tops are more common.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: On breakout from the diamond with volume.
- **Target**: The height of the diamond at its widest point, projected from breakout.
- **Success rate**: ~75% when occurred as a top pattern.

## 5. Harmonic Patterns

Harmonic patterns use Fibonacci ratios to identify precise reversal zones. Developed by H.M. Gartley (1935), expanded by Scott Carney, Larry Pesavento.

### 5.1 ABCD Pattern

**Description**: A 3-leg pattern (one pullback) that completes at a specific Fibonacci extension.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **AB leg**: Initial directional move.
2. **BC leg**: Retracement of AB (typically 61.8% or 78.6%).
3. **CD leg**: Extension of BC (typically 127.2% or 161.8%).
4. **Completion**: D is the reversal point.
5. **Harmonic completion**: CD should equal AB in time OR price.

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: At point D (the completion of CD).
- **Stop**: Beyond D by 1× ATR.
- **Target**: Three levels: 38.2%, 61.8%, 100% of CD.
- **Direction**: Bullish (AB up) or bearish (AB down).

### 5.2 Gartley (222) Pattern

**Description**: A 5-point reversal pattern (X-A-B-C-D) where D is within the X-A range.

**Identification Rules**:
1. **XA**: Initial move.
2. **B point**: Retracement to 61.8% of XA (key difference from Bat/Butterfly).
3. **C point**: Retracement between 38.2–88.6% of AB.
4. **D point**: Extension of BC to 78.6% of XA (no further than 88.6%).
5. D must be within the XA range (not beyond A).

**Trading Implications**:
- **Entry**: At D → 78.6% retracement of XA.
- **Stop**: Beyond X by 1× ATR.
- **Target**: 38.2%, 61.8%, 78.6% of AD.

### 5.3 Bat and Crab Patterns

**Bat Pattern**:
- **B point**: 38.2–50% retracement of XA.
- **D point**: 88.6% retracement of XA.
- **PRZ (Potential Reversal Zone)**: 88.6% XA + BC extension (161.8–261.8%).

**Crab Pattern**:
- **B point**: 38.2–61.8% retracement of XA.
- **D point**: 161.8% extension of XA (the crab extends BEYOND the XA range).
- **PRZ**: 161.8% XA + BC extension.
- **Note**: The crab is the most aggressive harmonic pattern — it signals an extreme reversal.

### 5.4 Butterfly Pattern

**Description**: Similar to Gartley but D extends beyond X (like the Crab but less extreme).

**Identification Rules**:
1. **B point**: 78.6% retracement of XA (deep retracement).
2. **D point**: 127.2–161.8% extension of XA.
3. **PRZ**: 127.2–161.8% XA + BC extension.

### 5.5 Shark and Cypher Patterns

**Shark Pattern** (developed by Scott Carney):
- **Key ratio**: 61.8% retracement of OX (a different labeling system).
- **C point**: 38.2–61.8% extension of AB.
- **D point**: 88.6% of XA (PRZ).

**Cypher Pattern**:
- **C point**: 61.8% retracement of XA.
- **D point**: 78.6% retracement of XC.
- **PRZ**: Tight zone around 78.6% of XC.

## 6. Candlestick Reversal Patterns

### 6.1 Single-Candle Reversals

**Doji** (uncertainty/reversal):
- Open and close at nearly the same price.
- Shape: horizontal body with long upper and/or lower wicks.
- **Gravestone Doji** (long upper wick): Bearish.
- **Dragonfly Doji** (long lower wick): Bullish.
- Market is indecisive. A doji after a trend is a potential reversal signal.
- **Confirmation**: Next candle determines direction.

**Hammer / Shooting Star**:
- **Hammer** (bullish reversal at bottom): Small body, long lower wick (2× body), little or no upper wick.
- **Shooting Star** (bearish reversal at top): Small body, long upper wick (2× body), little or no lower wick.
- Must appear after a downtrend (hammer) or uptrend (shooting star).
- The longer the wick, the more significant the reversal.
- **Volume**: Should be above average.

**Marubozu** (continuation):
- No wicks — full body candle.
- Open = low and close = high (bullish) or open = high and close = low (bearish).
- Indicates strong conviction in the direction.

**Spinning Top** (indecision):
- Small body with long upper and lower wicks.
- Appears during trends → potential pause.
- Alone is not a reversal signal; needs confirmation.

### 6.2 Two-Candle Reversals

**Engulfing Pattern**:
- **Bullish Engulfing**: Red (bearish) candle followed by a larger green (bullish) candle that completely "engulfs" the red candle's body.
- **Bearish Engulfing**: Green (bullish) candle followed by a larger red (bearish) candle that engulfs the green.
- Requirements: Clear prior trend, larger second candle, body-only engulfing (wicks can touch).
- **Reliability**: High. One of the most reliable candle patterns.
- **Volume**: Higher on the engulfing candle increases reliability.

**Harami** (inside candle):
- **Bullish Harami**: Large red candle followed by a small green candle completely inside the red candle's body.
- **Bearish Harami**: Large green candle followed by a small red candle inside the green body.
- Opposite of engulfing. Signals weakening momentum, potential reversal.
- Less reliable than engulfing.

**Piercing Pattern / Dark Cloud Cover**:
- **Piercing Pattern** (bullish): Red candle → green candle opens below red's low and closes above red's midpoint.
- **Dark Cloud Cover** (bearish): Green candle → red candle opens above green's high and closes below green's midpoint.
- The deeper the penetration, the more significant.

**Tweezer Top / Bottom**:
- Two candles with matching highs (tweezer top) or lows (tweezer bottom).
- First candle in trend direction, second candle that fails to extend.
- Confirms exhaustion of the trend move.

### 6.3 Three-Candle Reversals

**Morning Star** (bullish reversal):
1. Large red candle in a downtrend.
2. Small-bodied candle (doji or spinning top) that gaps below the first.
3. Large green candle closing at least halfway up the first red candle.
- **Perfect Morning Star**: The middle candle is a doji (gap on both sides).

**Evening Star** (bearish reversal):
1. Large green candle in an uptrend.
2. Small-bodied candle that gaps above the first.
3. Large red candle closing at least halfway down the first green candle.
- Most reliable at the end of extended trends.

**Three White Soldiers** (bullish continuation):
- Three consecutive long-bodied green candles with closes near highs.
- Each candle opens within the previous body and closes higher.
- Strong momentum. Trend is healthy.

**Three Black Crows** (bearish continuation):
- Three consecutive long-bodied red candles with closes near lows.
- Each opens within the previous body and closes lower.
- Strong selling momentum.

**Three Inside Up / Down**:
- **Three Inside Up**: Bullish engulfing → third candle makes a higher high above the second. More bullish than a simple engulfing.
- **Three Inside Down**: Bearish engulfing → third candle makes a lower low below the second.

## 7. Candlestick Continuation Patterns

**Rising Three Methods** (bullish continuation):
1. First candle: Long green.
2. Middle 3 candles: Small red candles within the first candle's range.
3. Last candle: Long green closing above the first candle's close.
- The three small candles are a "healthy pullback" within the trend.

**Falling Three Methods** (bearish continuation):
- Mirror: First red → three small green candles within range → final red close below.

**Matching High / Low**:
- Two consecutive candles with matching highs (bearish, resistance) or matching lows (bullish, support).

## 8. Elliott Wave Basics for Pattern Context

Elliott Wave Theory (R.N. Elliott, 1938) describes market movement in waves:

**Impulse waves** (trend direction): 5 waves → Waves 1, 3, 5 move with trend; Waves 2, 4 pull back. Wave 3 is longest, never the shortest.

**Corrective waves** (counter-trend): 3 waves (A-B-C) or more complex structures (triangles, flats, zigzags).

**Pattern placement within waves**:
- **Wave 1** or **Wave A**: May start with a double bottom, inverse H&S, or falling wedge.
- **Wave 3**: Flags or pennants in the middle of Wave 3.
- **Wave 5**: Ending diagonal, rising wedge, or bearish divergence. Last run.
- **Wave A / C**: Triangle, H&S, or other reversal patterns.

**Caution**: Elliott Wave is highly subjective. Different analysts will count the same chart differently. Use it as a context aid, not a precise trading tool.

## 9. Volume Confirmation by Pattern Type

| Pattern Type | Volume During Formation | Volume at Breakout | Volume After Breakout |
|---|---|---|---|
| **H&S** | Declines (left shoulder → head → right shoulder) | Spike (2×+) | Normal or declining on pullback |
| **Double top/bottom** | Lower on second peak/trough | Spike (2×+) | Normal |
| **Triangles** | Declines to apex | Spike (1.5×+) | Normal |
| **Flags/pennants** | Declining | Spike to flagpole level | Normal |
| **Wedges** | Declining | Spike | Normal |
| **Cup & handle** | Declining in handle | Spike | Normal |
| **Rectangles** | Variable | Spike | Normal |
| **Harmonic patterns** | Variable | Spike at D | Normal |

**No volume confirmation** → At least 50% probability the breakout is false. Do not trade.

## 11. Common Pattern Trading Mistakes

1. **Forcing patterns where none exist**. Not every three peaks is an H&S. If identification rules aren't met, it's not a pattern.
2. **Trading patterns without volume confirmation**. Low volume breakouts fail ~50% of the time.
3. **Taking every pattern signal**. Higher timeframes (4H+) produce more reliable patterns. Intraday patterns (< 15m) have high noise.
4. **Entering before the breakout closes**. An intra-breakout penetration that fails is a "spring" or "upthrust." Wait for the close.
5. **Ignoring the prior trend**. Continuation patterns work best when the prior trend is strong. Reversal patterns work best when the prior trend is extended.
6. **Moving stops too early**. Patterns often pull back after the initial breakout. Give the trade room.
7. **Holding too long after target is hit**. Pattern targets are measured moves — take profit when the target is reached.
8. **Confusing a range with a triangle**. A triangle has converging trendlines; a range has parallel ones. Different trading approaches apply.
9. **Using patterns in isolation**. Combine patterns with volume, EMAs, and regime detection for higher accuracy.
10. **Relying on failure rates without context**. A pattern with 80% success means 20% failure. Always risk-manage for the failure case.
